## Q8. Describe a time you troubleshot a complex data or system issue.

One of the most instructive troubleshooting experiences in my career came at **Morgan Stanley**, where I was asked to investigate a pattern of discrepancies between our **Power BI compliance dashboards** and the underlying source system records. Compliance stakeholders had noticed that certain risk metric values were not matching what they expected based on their manual checks, and the discrepancy was appearing inconsistently — some report cycles were clean, others had multiple mismatches. I approached this systematically, starting by documenting the exact nature and magnitude of each discrepancy I could observe. I then traced the data flow from source to dashboard, examining each transformation step in the **SQL-based data pipeline** to identify where the divergence was being introduced. I discovered that the issue originated in how one of the **4 upstream systems** was exporting timestamp data — the system was using a different timezone convention than the others, which caused records near day boundaries to be assigned to the wrong reporting period depending on when the export ran. I documented the root cause clearly, implemented a **timezone normalisation step** in the pipeline, and added a **data validation rule** specifically targeting this edge case to prevent recurrence. After the fix, discrepancies dropped from approximately **15 flagged records per cycle to fewer than 3**. At **Dell Technologies** I similarly conducted **root-cause analyses** on discrepancies between **ERP outputs** and downstream reporting tables, identifying **6 systematic mapping errors**. In both cases my approach was the same: resist the temptation to apply a quick patch, trace the issue to its true source, document what I found, implement a systematic fix, and add monitoring to detect recurrence. For the **CID** role, I would apply this same disciplined approach to troubleshooting issues within **Knack**, **HearForm**, and the **Power BI** reporting environment.
