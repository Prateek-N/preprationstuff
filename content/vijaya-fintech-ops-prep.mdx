## Q3. Tell me about a time you identified a process gap and drove an improvement.

One of the clearest examples from my career at **Honeywell** involved the company's vendor contract renewal process. When I took ownership of contract management across **12 vendor agreements**, I discovered there was no systematic process for tracking renewal dates, flagging **auto-renewal clauses**, or monitoring **price escalators** between renewal cycles. Contracts were being reviewed reactively — often only when a renewal was imminent — which meant the team had limited leverage to negotiate and sometimes missed opportunities to consolidate overlapping subscriptions. I identified this gap through a structured review of all active contracts and documented the problem with specific examples of where the absence of a proactive process had cost the organisation negotiating power. I then designed and implemented a solution: a **Claude API pipeline** with **engineered extraction prompts** integrated into **Asana** and **Confluence** workflows that automatically extracted **15 structured fields** from each contract, flagged renewal dates, auto-renewal risks, and price escalation clauses, and routed items requiring action to the appropriate owner. This eliminated the need for manual contract reading for routine reviews, cut review time by **2 to 3 hours per contract**, and enabled a **subscription consolidation initiative** that saved **$18,000 annually**. At **Adani** I identified a similar gap in the reporting infrastructure — multiple teams were producing overlapping reports from different systems, creating reconciliation workload and inconsistency risk. I developed a **reporting consolidation business case** including **cost estimates**, **ROI analysis**, **implementation effort**, **budget impact**, and **risk assessment** that enabled leadership to select the optimal migration strategy. In both cases my approach was the same: measure the current state, quantify the impact of the gap, design a systematic solution, and document it clearly enough that it could be maintained and improved by others. This is exactly the approach I would bring to identifying and closing **process gaps** at this FinTech.

## Q14. How do you approach root cause analysis when an operational issue arises?

**Root cause analysis** is a methodology I apply systematically whenever an operational issue recurs or has significant business impact, and I have used it to identify and resolve problems ranging from data discrepancies to capacity planning bottlenecks. At **Honeywell** I applied structured **root cause analysis** to a capacity planning problem across **4 manufacturing lines**, where initial analysis suggested the constraint was insufficient overall capacity. By using **Advanced Excel** models and **Jira** log data to isolate cost variables, I identified that the actual bottleneck was a **shared-resource constraint** — a specific piece of equipment used by multiple lines was creating a scheduling conflict that inflated apparent capacity requirements. This finding changed the recommended solution entirely: rather than authorising expensive overtime or hiring, I recommended a targeted line rebalancing that addressed the shared-resource conflict, which was the lowest-cost option by a significant margin. At **Adani** I conducted **root cause analysis** on data reconciliation issues across **3 reporting systems** fed by **850,000+ SAP ERP transactions**, tracing discrepancies through the data transformation logic to identify where figures diverged from source system records. My approach in both cases was the same: resist the temptation to accept the first plausible explanation; trace the issue systematically through each processing step; document what I find at each stage; and distinguish between the symptom (the observable problem) and the cause (what produced it). For this FinTech role, I would apply the same analytical rigour to operational issue investigation — using data to trace problems to their source, documenting findings clearly, and proposing systemic fixes rather than temporary patches.
